\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Cross-language semantic equivalence}

Java and Rust consumed the same literal 30-trial workload containing 30
targets, 30,720 Hellman starts, and 30,720 distinguished-point starts. The
complete deterministic CSV outputs were byte-for-byte identical, including
target ciphertexts, exact coverage, endpoint counts, recovery decisions, and
lookup counters.

On that workload, Hellman recovered \SharedHellmanSuccess{} targets and had
median exact coverage \SharedHellmanCoverage{}. The distinguished-point table
recovered \SharedDPSuccess{} targets and had median exact coverage
\SharedDPCoverage{}. These are correctness results; no cross-language timing
claim is inferred.

\subsection{Finite-size residuals are shared by random controls}

The Ma--Hong recurrence is asymptotic, so small residuals are expected at
finite $N$. In the sweep, residuals of similar scale also appear in the
random-map controls. The configuration with the largest absolute paired mean
DES-minus-random difference is \LargestDeltaConfig{}, at
\LargestPairedMeanPP{} percentage points.

Within the tested dimensions and parameters, this provides no evidence that
the DES-derived mapping has coverage behavior qualitatively different from the
finite random mappings.

\subsection{Chain shape matters at fixed nominal budget}

The equal-budget $b=16$ family fixes $mt=N$ while varying $t$.
Table~\ref{tab:hellman-main-results} shows that coverage is not determined by
$mt$ alone. Shorter rows and more starts cover a larger fraction of the state
space; longer rows undergo more permanent coalescence under the fixed map.
Both DES and random-map measurements follow the trend predicted by the
Ma--Hong recurrence.

\subsection{What the experiments establish}

Within the reduced state spaces tested here, the evidence supports three
specific claims. First, Java and Rust agree on the full deterministic semantics
of the committed shared workload. Second, exact Hellman coverage of the
DES-derived mappings is close to both the Ma--Hong random-function prediction
and matched finite random-map controls. Third, distinguished-point truncation
and coverage can be measured exactly and compared with both fresh-sample and
finite-map references.

These claims are deliberately narrower than saying that DES itself is a random
function. The tested object is the reduced mapping $F_b$ induced by the chosen
key embedding, fixed plaintext, DES primitive, and reduction.
