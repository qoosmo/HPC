\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Classical time--memory tradeoffs.}
Hellman introduced the cryptanalytic time--memory tradeoff for block-cipher key
recovery and analyzed the effect of chain collisions under random-function
assumptions~\cite{Hellman1980}.  Fiat and Naor later gave rigorous time/space
tradeoffs for function inversion beyond the random-function setting
~\cite{FiatNaor1991}.  Barkan, Biham, and Shamir developed a general model of
cryptanalytic TMTOs based on stateful random graphs and proved rigorous bounds
covering classical and rainbow-style constructions~\cite{BarkanBihamShamir2006}.
Recent work continues to revisit the fundamental limits of preprocessing and
function inversion~\cite{GolovnevEtAl2023}.

\paragraph{Random mappings and coverage.}
Iteration chains naturally induce functional graphs.  Flajolet and Odlyzko
systematically analyzed random-mapping statistics including cycles,
components, and related structural parameters~\cite{FlajoletOdlyzko1990}.
For Hellman tables specifically, Ma and Hong studied the expected coverage
rate and supplied a recurrence that closely matches experiments under a random
function model~\cite{MaHong2009}.  Their framework is a central baseline for
the present paper: our goal is not to replace it, but to test it against exact
coverage of a concrete finite cryptographic mapping.

\paragraph{Rainbow and perfect tables.}
Oechslin's rainbow construction changes the reduction across chain positions,
reducing the conditions under which collisions turn into permanent chain
merges~\cite{Oechslin2003}.  Avoine, Junod, and Oechslin analyzed perfect
classic, distinguished-point, and rainbow tables, including practical
performance considerations~\cite{AvoineJunodOechslin2008}.  These works make
clear that coverage, preprocessing cost, and lookup cost must be compared at
matched success probabilities rather than through nominal table sizes alone.

\paragraph{False alarms and distinguished points.}
Endpoint matches do not always imply successful recovery: false alarms can
force regeneration of candidate chains and materially affect the online cost.
Hong analyzed this cost for Hellman and rainbow tradeoffs~\cite{Hong2008}.
Lee and Hong compared major perfect-table variants and developed detailed
analysis for distinguished-point constructions~\cite{LeeHong2012}.

\paragraph{Position of this work.}
The present paper focuses on reproducibility at finite state sizes where the
entire mapping and table coverage can be enumerated.  This permits a direct
three-way comparison among established random-function predictions, a concrete
DES-derived mapping, and independent Java/Rust implementations.  The value of
the study is therefore in exact instrumentation, controlled comparisons, and
reproducible validation---not in reintroducing known TMTO phenomena.
